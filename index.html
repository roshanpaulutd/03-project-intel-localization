Sustainability Through the Ages

Explore Intel's journey through time, discovering how our commitment to innovation has shaped a more sustainable future for technology and our planet.

1968 to today

Innovation with a lighter footprint

Scroll across the milestones on larger screens. On smaller screens, the story stacks for easy reading.

1968

Intel Founded

Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore rename the newly formed company NM Electronics to Intel Corporation, laying the foundation for decades of technological innovation.

Intel's beginning established a culture of solving difficult engineering problems. That spirit later helped the company take on the environmental challenges created by semiconductor manufacturing.

1971

First Microprocessor

Intel debuts the 4004, the world's first commercial microprocessor, igniting the microprocessor revolution and propelling the future of computing devices.

The 4004 showed how much computing power could fit into a tiny piece of silicon. More efficient chips would eventually help computers do more work while using less energy.

1978

8086 Processor

Launch of the 8086 processor, establishing the x86 architecture that drives countless PCs and servers in the modern era.

The x86 architecture became a lasting foundation for personal computing. Its long life is also a reminder that durable technology can reduce the need to replace hardware as often.

1985

386 Processor

Intel introduces the 386 processor with 32-bit architecture, ushering in a new era of performance and multitasking for personal computers.

The 386 made multitasking more practical for everyday computer users. It represents how advances in chip design can expand access to useful technology.

2006

Peak GHG Emissions

This year marks Intel's highest annual greenhouse gas emissions for operations. Over subsequent years, Intel invests heavily in chemical abatement, renewable energy, and energy-efficient manufacturing to reverse this trend.

This milestone recognizes a problem as well as a turning point. Intel's response included better chemical abatement, more efficient manufacturing, and greater use of renewable electricity.

2020

RISE Strategy

Intel launches its RISE (Responsible, Inclusive, Sustainable, Enabling) strategy and 2030 goals, aiming to drive industry-wide progress on climate action, water stewardship, and waste reduction.

RISE brings environmental responsibility together with inclusion and enabling technology. Its 2030 goals give the company measurable targets instead of treating sustainability as a side project.

2022

Net-Zero By 2040

Intel announces its commitment to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions (Scope 1 and 2) across its global operations by 2040, building on years of environmental initiatives.

The net-zero commitment focuses on Scope 1 and 2 emissions from global operations. Reaching it will require continued efficiency work, renewable energy, and careful planning across Intel's sites.

2023

Renewable Electricity

The company achieves 99% renewable electricity usage worldwide, helping to drastically lower carbon emissions and driving progress toward Intel's long-term sustainability goals.

Using 99% renewable electricity worldwide is a major operational step. It shows how purchasing and generating cleaner energy can complement improvements inside factories.

2024

Sustainability Summit

Intel hosts its first Sustainability Summit, uniting suppliers, government officials, and industry leaders to collaborate on next-generation sustainable semiconductor manufacturing.

The summit makes sustainability a shared industry challenge. Bringing suppliers, government, and technology leaders together can help move better ideas from one organization into the wider semiconductor ecosystem.
